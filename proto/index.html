Seven ways it could actually work.

The same product and the same week of posts, built seven times. Every screen is clickable — answer a held question, edit a caption, approve the week, open the library and the settings. Built phone-first; they work on desktop too.

Try it on your phone. The buyer is a cafe owner checking this for five minutes between customers, so that is the screen that matters.

Bold

Graphic and loud

Thick borders, hard shadows, whole cards filled with coral and marigold. The week colour-codes itself so you can read it in one glance from across the counter.

Open it → Receipt

A printed ticket

The week as a paper receipt from the counter. Torn edges, stamped marks, itemised like a bill. The most distinctive of the three, and the one nobody else looks like.

Open it → Conversation

A message thread

The week arrives as messages. Held questions come with tappable answers. Nothing to learn for someone who already runs her shop through WhatsApp.

Open it → Glass

Lit from behind

Bold rebuilt in frosted glass. Because glass only reads against something, the coral and marigold move behind the panels and become the light instead of the surface — same content, same flow, one CSS layer different.

Open it → Workshop

Warm and unhurried

The loud version rebuilt in a calmer hand — cream canvas, one coral for action, big rounded surfaces, and headlines set light instead of heavy. Same screens, quieter voice.

Open it → The queue

Not a dashboard at all

Built from what she actually has — five minutes, one thumb, between customers. It opens on the thing that needs her, one question at a time, and ends when there is nothing left. The week, the library and the history are still there, underneath, for when she wants them.

Open it → Golden hour

Her photographs, big

Warm parchment, headlines set light rather than heavy, no shadows anywhere, and the food photography carrying the screen instead of sitting in thumbnails. The most finished of the seven.
